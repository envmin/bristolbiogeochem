A virtual mineral laboratory

In Mineral Detective you take the role of an analytical scientist, working out what unknown samples are made of with the same techniques used in research laboratories around the world. Collect clues, prepare specimens, operate instruments, interpret spectra and build your case from the evidence.

Investigate like a real scientist

Most science simulations skip straight to the answer. Mineral Detective focuses on the process.

You will see why sample preparation matters, how analytical instruments work, and how small mistakes change the data you collect. Over-grind a sample, mount it carelessly, choose the wrong measurement settings or misread a spectrum, and the evidence may lead you astray.

Four real analytical techniques

X-ray fluorescence

XRF

Find out which elements are present and gather your first clues about the sample.

X-ray diffraction

XRD

Prepare powders, mount them correctly, collect diffraction patterns and identify crystalline minerals from their atomic structure.

Mössbauer spectroscopy

⁵⁷Fe

Probe the iron atoms directly, revealing their oxidation state and the minerals they sit in. It is one of the most powerful techniques for iron-bearing materials.

Infrared spectroscopy

FTIR

Identify carbonates, hydroxides, silicates and other mineral groups from the characteristic vibrations of their chemical bonds.

Crack the case

Begin with pure reference minerals to learn how each technique behaves. Then take on mystery samples that contain several minerals, incomplete clues and competing interpretations. Only by combining the evidence from several techniques can you reveal the full story.

Who is it for?

  • Earth sciences students
  • Geochemistry and environmental science students
  • Researchers learning mineral characterisation
  • Teachers looking for interactive teaching resources
  • Anyone curious about how scientists identify unknown materials

No experience is needed, but every step is grounded in the analytical workflows used in real research laboratories.

Built by scientists

Mineral Detective was developed by Environmental Mineralogy, James Byrne's research group in the School of Earth Sciences at the University of Bristol. It helps students and researchers explore mineral characterisation through hands-on investigation. Its models include realistic sample preparation, instrument operation and interpretation challenges inspired by real laboratory practice.
